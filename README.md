# Liability-Driven Behavior Framework (LDBF) Revision 2.0

## Introduction: The Illusion of Moral Depth

Human action is observed, interpreted, and narrated. Moral language such as loyalty, duty, devotion, strategy compresses into a projection: the normative plane.

A man performs deeds to honor his god. A mother hides a son who committed murder. A strategist defies convention to advance ambition. An individual commits suicide. These stories fall into a two-dimensional normative moral plane:

* Quadrant II: Canonically praised, alignment on both axes.

* Quadrant I: Self-justified deviation, personal code preserved against external condemnation.

* Quadrant IV: Dutiful conformity, obligation without internal endorsement.

* Quadrant III: The condemned abyss, no internal or external justification. Suicide. Collapse. Oblivion.

Why does righteousness matter? The urge to be right inflates Quadrant II. It fills stories, laws, and traditions. Yet the space of Quadrant III lies almost empty; acts without internal or external justification exist only as theoretical ghosts. The obsession with being aligned, with being praised, is a projection. The machinery of action neither honors nor condemns. Survival, extraction, horizon, and liability decide, indifferent to approval. The plane catalogs only appearances. It does not govern behavior.

History demonstrates the failure of normative implications. Practices once deemed ethical such as child marriage, slavery, burning widows alive were defended as righteous. Values derived from normative moral imperatives lag behind the machinery of action. They are weak and submit to the machinery when they inevitably reconstitute under pressure.

Beneath narrative lies the machinery. Horizons, asymmetric estimation, profit, and power govern all behavior. Grey areas, loyalty, ambition, deviance are mechanics. LDBF does not predict, it unearths the primitives of volatile morality.

### Axioms

**Axiom 0 (Entropy).**
Entropy degrades power, expands liabilities, and compresses private horizons, contracting the set of reachable utility outcomes.

**Axiom 1 (Utility, Profit, and Power).**
Utility is the sole scalar objective. Profit is realized utility: the asset–liability delta. Power is the capacity to determine which profit realizations are possible by reshaping action sets, enforcement, and liability under uncertainty. Power is not utility and is not reducible to profit.

**Axiom 2 (Agents and Actors).**
An agent is any system capable of producing a utility delta. An actor is an agent capable of realizing utility and updating strategy. Selection occurs exclusively at the actor level.

**Axiom 3 (Harm).**

Harm is imposed negative utility delta. It has no intrinsic moral status and represents a state transition only.

**Axiom 4 (Time and Horizon).**
Time is variable. Actors operate over private, non-observable horizons. Actions may be locally profitable and globally unprofitable without contradiction.

**Axiom 5 (Liability).**
Liability is expected future negative utility conditional on action. Law, retaliation, reputation, and institutions function as liability mechanisms.

**Axiom 6 (Optimization).**
Actors choose actions that maximize expected discounted utility given realizability, horizon, liability surface, and uncertainty.

**Axiom 7 (Power Accumulation).**
Power accumulates as insurance against profit decay. Latent power preserves optionality under uncertainty and induces persistent asymmetries in estimation.

**Axiom 8 (Compositions).**
Actors do not merge into a single agent. Institutions are compositions of actors temporarily aligned by incentives. Defection remains continuously available.

**Axiom 9 (Politics).**
Politics is the engineering of power and liability distributions to shape behavior. Psychology is internal politics among competing sub-agents.

**Axiom 10 (Stability and End Game).**
No permanent equilibrium exists. All compositions are temporally stable and degrade toward predation as information spreads and liabilities weaken.
